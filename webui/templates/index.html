⚡ Flash me — first-time setup over USB

Use this once per cube, the very first time you connect it. Plugs the cube into your Mac, writes your WiFi credentials into the cube's flash, and installs the OTA listener so future updates can go wirelessly. After this, you'll never need the USB cable for that cube again unless something goes badly wrong.

What gets flashed and why?

The Kublet runs bare-metal ESP32 firmware — there is no Linux, no shell, no SSH. What's "running" on it is whatever firmware was last loaded:

  • Out of the box ("open mobile app" screen): Kublet's original closed firmware. It waits for the dead iOS app to pair via BLE. It does NOT accept OTA pushes from us, so we have to replace it.
  • After this Flash Me step: the dev firmware from the markusos community fork. It joins your WiFi using the credentials you enter below, and listens on port 80 of its LAN IP for app uploads.
  • After your first app deploy (e.g. silver): that app's firmware replaces the dev firmware — but the dev firmware's OTA listener gets bundled into the app, so further updates still work over WiFi.

WiFi credentials are written into the cube's NVS flash (namespace core, keys ssid and pw). They live there even across power cycles and firmware swaps. They never leave your machine.

🔌 Cube not showing up over USB? Run a diagnostic

The Kublet has a USB Mini-B port (the older trapezoid connector — not modern USB-C). If your cube's screen says "open mobile app" or shows the Kublet logo, the cable is delivering power. If it isn't appearing at /dev/cu.* for flashing, the most likely causes are:

  1. Charge-only cable. A lot of old USB-Mini cables never had the data pins wired. Try a different one — preferably a cable you know syncs an actual device, not just charges it.
  2. Missing CP210x driver. The cube's USB-serial chip is a Silicon Labs CP210x. Without the driver, macOS can power the cube but doesn't expose a serial node. Install from silabs.com (CP210x VCP for macOS), then reboot.
  3. USB hub. Some hubs don't pass through serial devices cleanly. Plug the cube directly into the Mac.

ℹ About your Kublet & this fleet

    

      

        The Kublet cube is a
        small ESP32-based 240×240 LCD desk display funded on Kickstarter. The
        company went silent in August 2024: developer portal offline, no responses
        to issues, no firmware updates. The hardware works fine; we just need our
        own toolchain to keep it useful.
      

      
What this fleet manager does

      
    
        
  • Tracks each cube by name, IP, and a personal number (#N)
            shown in the corner of every app.
    • 
        
  • Builds the firmware for any app in kublet-apps/apps/
            with that cube's KUBLET_NUM baked in via
            -DKUBLET_NUM=N, then OTA-pushes it.
    • 
        
  • Handles the one-time USB flash too (Flash Me section above).
    • 
        
  • Runs on port 1666. Bundled as a Docker image so it
            can live on a Pi or NAS and you don't need a Mac terminal open.
    • 
      

      
What's actually inside the cube

      
    
        
  • SoC: ESP32 (240 MHz, 320 KB RAM, ~1.3 MB flash for app firmware)
    • 
        
  • Display: ST7789, 240×240, RGB 565 over SPI @ 40 MHz
    • 
        
  • Backlight: GPIO 15, PWM via ESP32 ledc
    • 
        
  • Button: GPIO 19, active LOW
    • 
        
  • WiFi: 2.4 GHz only (ESP32 limitation)
    • 
        
  • Other peripherals: none documented or wired up.
            No IMU, no temperature sensor, no microphone — just display + button.
    • 
      

      
OTA protocol

      

        Once the dev firmware is on, the cube exposes POST /update
        on port 80 of its LAN IP. Multipart upload of firmware.bin;
        no auth, no encryption. If you care about that, put your kublets on a
        VLAN separate from your other traffic.
      

      
Documentation we recovered

      

        The original developer portal (developers.thekublet.com) is
        offline but cached on the Wayback Machine.
        The most useful pages are mirrored locally at
        ../source_archive/wayback-developers-portal/.
